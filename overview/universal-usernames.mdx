## Two ways teams create usernames today

Almost every product that ships usernames picks one of two approaches. Both leave the user stuck at the same place.

**Plain usernames:** usually displayed like `@alice` or `alice`. Fast to build, and inside your app they work exactly as intended. But the name only exists in your database. Send `@alice` to someone in another wallet or app, and it simply won't work. Users are back to copying and pasting an address.

**A custom TLD:** something like `alice.wallet` or `alice.pay`, whatever reads well next to your brand name. This looks like the better option but it's not. Your TLD isn't in DNS and isn't in ENS, so no wallet outside your product has any way to look it up. Making it work means asking every wallet, explorer, and app to integrate your naming system one at a time. Also, collisions are the other half of the problem: multiple projects have built competing versions of the same TLD, so `alice.wallet` can resolve to two different people depending on which app you ask.

## A better way with ENS

Every username you've already issued gets a matching ENS name under a `.eth` name you own. One rule, applied across your whole user base:

```
Your username:    happy
Your .eth name:   brand.eth
ENS name:         happy.brand.eth
```

Your system stays the source of truth and no data moves. The username just gains a form that ENS-aware wallets already know how to read.

<Note>
This does not create a new ENS top-level domain. Each user gets an ENS name under your `.eth` name for the identity they already have.
</Note>

## How it works

You run a small HTTPS endpoint that returns a JSON profile for one username: display name, avatar, other text records, and addresses. You connect `brand.eth` to that endpoint in the Namespace App, then point `brand.eth` at Namespace's HybridResolverV2 resolver.

From then on, a lookup for `happy.brand.eth` works like this:

1. The wallet asks ENS. HybridResolverV2 has no record stored for `happy`, so it answers with a CCIP-Read lookup that sends the wallet to the Namespace gateway.
2. The gateway calls your endpoint for `happy`, checks the response, and signs the record the wallet asked for.
3. HybridResolverV2 checks the signature and its expiry on Ethereum before the wallet uses the answer.

Nobody claims, mints, or registers anything per user, and nothing is written onchain per user. If a username exists in your system, it resolves. If you rename, suspend, or reassign it there, Namespace serves the change within 60 seconds. Wallets and apps can cache answers for longer.

One name can hold an address for every chain your users hold funds on, including non-EVM chains such as Bitcoin and Solana, next to profile fields like display name and avatar. Your endpoint returns all of them in one response.

## Your records stay where they are

Your endpoint reads from the database or service you run today. Namespace doesn't copy your user list or store profiles. The gateway keeps each response for at most 60 seconds, so a burst of lookups for one user costs your endpoint one request.

## Who controls what

Your system decides which usernames exist, what their records say, and who each name points to. Users don't get independent ownership of their name, so they can't transfer it or edit it outside your product.

Only the wallet that owns `brand.eth` can connect it, replace the endpoint, pause it, or disconnect it. Each change needs a signature from that wallet but no transaction.

Everything that resolves is public. Anyone can look up `happy.brand.eth` and read its records, even when your endpoint requires a bearer token from Namespace.

## Decide before you build

1. **The parent name.** A `.eth` name you own directly below `.eth`, such as `brand.eth`. Each username becomes one label under it.
2. **The endpoint.** The service that answers `GET /{username}` with a profile, and whether it requires a bearer token.
3. **Who runs it.** Someone holds the parent name, keeps the endpoint online, and fixes it when it breaks.

## What this does not do

- The parent has to be a `.eth` name directly below `.eth`. DNS names imported into ENS and deeper names such as `team.brand.eth` can't be connected.
- Each username is a single label. `happy.brand.eth` works; `team.happy.brand.eth` doesn't.
- Records come from your endpoint over HTTPS. They aren't read from a contract, an L2, or another chain.
- Reverse resolution isn't supported, so an address can't show `happy.brand.eth` as its primary name.
- Support varies. Most major wallets resolve these names, but not every app does, so test the ones your users actually use.
