## Why it's needed

Your users already have a username, like `@happy`. Inside your app it works: people send money to it, find each other with it, and see it on leaderboards.

Outside your app, it doesn't exist. A friend in another wallet can't pay `@happy`, so they ask for an address and paste `0x71C7…F2a9`. That's slow, and one wrong character sends the money somewhere it can't come back from.

Naming services with their own TLD run into the same wall. `happy.brand` only resolves in the apps that integrated it, and every other wallet has to be convinced one at a time.

## What Universal Usernames does

Every username you've issued gets a matching ENS name, under one rule:

```
Your username:   happy
ENS name:        happy.brand.eth
        or       happy.brand.id, using a DNS domain imported into ENS
```

Wallets, explorers and apps that already read ENS can resolve it, with no integration on their side. You don't move users, mint names or sync records. Your system stays the source of truth.

## How it works

You build one HTTP endpoint that takes a username and returns that user's public profile: addresses, avatar and other records. Then you point your ENS name at Namespace. When a wallet looks up `happy.brand.eth`, Namespace calls your endpoint, signs the answer and hands it back.

<FlowExplainer />

The endpoint reads from wherever your usernames live. A neobank, wallet or payments app reads its own database. A naming service with names onchain reads its registry contract or indexer. Both return the same JSON, so the rest of the setup is identical.

## Decide before you build

- **The parent name.** `brand.eth`, or a domain you already own. Usernames need to be valid lowercase ENS labels.
- **What's public.** Anyone can look up any username and read every record you return. Return `404` for accounts that shouldn't be found.
- **Who keeps it running.** Your endpoint's uptime is the name's uptime. There's no fallback copy.
- **Who holds the parent name.** Whoever controls `brand.eth` can redirect every username.

## What it doesn't do

- `.brand` doesn't become an ENS TLD. Users gain an ENS name alongside the username they have.
- Users don't own these names onchain. The name follows your system, so you can rename or remove it.
- Most major wallets follow CCIP-Read, but not every app does. Test the ones your users rely on.
